## Summary

The L1 architecture is approved. The design resolves OD-4…OD-8 inside their bounding requirements, honors the settled OD-1…OD-3 without reopening them, and enforces the safety delta (a)–(d) as invariants rather than aspirations. Every load-bearing seam claim I spot-checked holds in the worktree code: the scheduler's persist-then-arm order (`MedicationScheduler.swift` :733–744), the challenge-on-first-call behaviour (:254), the denial-first gate order (`CommandRouter.swift` :2491 before :2497), the double-dose detector's entry-and-window-scoped contract with no dose input (`DoubleDoseDetector.swift` :21), the ack-window/re-fire/escalation derivation from one `Date` (`EscalationEngine.swift` :29–75, `recover` :171–208), the unwired-but-preserved `PhotoVerifier` (zero call sites outside its own file, zero tests), the honest-to-change push stub (`FamilyNotifier.swift` :222 `return true`), the candidate-not-binding scan path (`MedicationEditorDraft.applyScan` :69, `MedicationOCR.swift` header), the scanner's offload and honest outcomes, `IdHashing.shortHash` as a truncated one-way SHA-256, `CalendarShareKey.oneOff` and the shipped consent gate (`CalendarShareMapper.canShare` :196), the `onScheduleChanged` → `reconcileMedication` wiring, the `SpokenTime` formatter precedents, the `[ACK-MIC-CONTINUITY]` fast-lane property, the release log gate wired into `ios/build.sh`, and all fourteen scoped test suites named in the constitution. All 22 FR-MSR and 10 NFR-MSR map to substantive architecture sections (§22), no requirement is silently weakened or deferred without a boundary, and no scope is invented beyond the requirements and the OD-4…OD-8 boundaries the requirements set.

One disclosed defect is material and must be resolved before implementation claims consent compliance: the design's calendar invitee deviation (significant-event entries ignore the first-fire sweep it says is bound to the consent copy the household sees — see M-1). The design itself flags this surface in §23 as its top review focus; this review turns that flag into a binding, bounded resolution rather than a "should be reviewed".

## Findings

### Blockers

None.

### Major

**M-1 — Calendar significant-event invitations exceed the shipped consent disclosure (design §9.3; §23 risk 1).**

Evidence:

- Shipped disclosure copy (`ios/ElderlyAssistant/Resources/Localizable.xcstrings`): `calendarShare.consent.whoCanSee` [en] reads "Emergency contacts are always invited. Other family members are invited for the kinds of reminders you switch on in Notify caregivers." (Nepali mirrors it.) `calendarShare.consent.body` [en] discloses the content: "Event titles, including medicine names, will be sent to your Google Calendar and shared with invited family contacts."
- Design §9.3's consent paragraph asserts the event entries stay in the disclosed content class "(medication name + time, to the same invitees)", and then two paragraphs later, under "Invitee rule (deviation recorded)", plans the opposite audience: significant-event entries "use the family-notification contact set (contacts with an email, de-duplicated, emergency-first …) without consulting the first-fire opt-in".
- Code confirming the shipped audience rule: `Services/CalendarSync/CalendarShareMapper.swift` :155 `inviteeEmails` suppresses non-emergency contacts for `.medicationReminder` unless `CaregiverNotifySettings.isEnabled(for: .medicationReminder)` is on; :214 `medicationDrafts` feeds the mirror through it; :233 the mirrored title is the bare medication name.

Effect: under the deviation, a household with every notify switch off would have non-emergency contacts (with an email) invited to missed-dose, possible-double-dose and escalation-started calendar entries, although the disclosure those contacts' household accepted says other family members are invited only for switched-on reminder kinds. The design cannot both claim the consent text covers this and keep the deviation on the current copy.

Required resolution (bounded; settle in design-l2, inside the fixed L1 mechanism): either (i) align significant-event calendar invitees with the shipped suppression rule (emergency contacts always; others per the notify switches), which needs no copy change, or (ii) amend the consent copy (`calendarShare.consent.whoCanSee` and any adjacent strings) in BOTH locales to state that significant medication events are shared with all family contacts who have an email. Until one of these lands, §9.3's consent-coverage sentence is inaccurate and must not be relied on at implementation.

### Minor

**m-1 — `rearmForSlotClockChange()` declares no error outcome (design §4.5, §21.1, §14).** The new `func rearmForSlotClockChange()` returns Void; its only failure channel is the shipped `reminder_persistence_failed` observability event (`MedicationScheduler.swift` :781–792). The design-agent principle ("every interface method must declare explicit error return types") and the design's own §9.5 honesty rule ("a failed persistence write … surfaces") argue for a returned outcome (per-entry skips or `Result<Void, SafetyError>`) so the mapping-save caller can surface skipped arming on the settings surface. `medicationAckCandidates()` as a pure in-memory read whose empty case is expressed by the `AckResolution` enum is acceptable as designed.

**m-2 — Duplicate-entry semantics for slot-model saves is unspecified (design §19 `addMedication` row).** The shipped duplicate guard intersects `entry.scheduleTimes` (`AppCoordinator.swift` :11641–11645). The slot model gives `scheduleTimes` no semantics for slot entries (§11: "the slot model never consults `scheduleTimes` for slot entries"), so unless the save plumbing is redesigned, the guard silently stops flagging a same-name entry at the same slot. Not safety-blocking (no FR requires the guard), but design-l2 must state the rule it wants.

**m-3 — Scanned-schedule-to-slot conversion is phrased at word level while the parser emits clock hours (design §6).** The parser's outputs are `DateComponents` from `MedicationLabelOCR.swift` `timeWordHours` :203, `slotHours` :213 and `countedDoseHours` :220. The design's table ("morning → morning; midday/noon/afternoon → afternoon; evening/night → evening") is derivable but should be pinned at the level the implementation consumes: 8:00 → morning; 12:00/13:00 → afternoon; 19:00/20:00/21:00 → evening, deduplicated when a read names several evening words. The boundary is already fixed (convention hours, never the household mapping; an unconvertible item fills nothing) — pinning the hour table in design-l2 closes the gap.

**m-4 — Clarification walk: the "no" behaviour for three or more candidates is unspecified (design §7.3).** For the pair form, a plain "no" ends the walk; for the single-candidate form, "no" ends with nothing recorded; for three or more candidates ("single-candidate form from cursor 0 onward") the design does not say whether "no" advances the cursor or ends the walk. FR-MSR-008's determinism scenario holds under either rule, but the choice should be pinned in design-l2 so the walk and its copy are testable end to end.

**m-5 — Retime refusal: family visibility and event vocabulary (design §11, §12).** The design refuses `setScheduleTimes` for slot entries (`MedicationScheduler.swift` :152; relay at `AppCoordinator.swift` :12067; mutation planning at `CalendarSyncService.swift` :617–690 detects family retimes in the Calendar app) with "an honest observability event" and the statement "the family edits the slot in the app instead". No family-visible surface carries that outcome (observability events are operator-facing), and the event is not among §12's named new event strings; the forward mirror path re-asserts the app's times on the next pass, so in practice the family's calendar edit is undone without explanation. design-l2 should either design a family-visible line (settings status / in-app note) or record the accepted quiet-revert behaviour explicitly, and name the event in the §12 vocabulary.

### Notes

**n-1 — Simple-ack fired-preference (design §7.2 rule 4; §23 risk 2) — explicit judgment: accepted.** A bare "taken" with several pending reminders prefers the reminders that have rung (`fired == true`) before walking clarification. FR-MSR-007's failure scenario is about several medications sharing a slot (same mapped time — so if any rung, both are in the fired set and the outcome is clarification) and its edge scenario pins only "exactly one reminder pending". The fired-preference is a conservative, deterministic reading of "unambiguous", the single-pending case is byte-identical to today's behaviour, and any misread still passes the FR-D01 challenge, whose prompt names the medication — a wrong binding costs a correction, not a wrong record. Recorded as a design-level reading for review; accepted.

**n-2 — A mapping change whose new time has already passed today replaces the un-fired occurrence with tomorrow's (design §4.5 step 3, "the standard creation rules (nextOccurrence from now)").** This matches the shipped retime path's semantics (`createPendingReminders` :707 → `nextOccurrence` :747 when called after the new time). Shipped-equivalent and deterministic; design-l2 should confirm it is intended for the mapping-change surface too, since "the family changes the mapping" and "the family retimes one dose" are not obviously the same act.

**n-3 — Citation drift is present but orientation-level, as the document itself declares.** Spot-checked drifts: `startVoiceAckConfirmation` cited :12657, actual :12664; `router.noPendingReminder` cited :5384, actual :5387; editor save cited :4673, actual :4674; `todayMedicationSummaryLines` cited :12625 (that is the `SpokenTime` call line; the function opens :12619). Every drift is ≤8 lines and every symbol named in the document exists and behaves as claimed — no seam claim failed on content.

**n-4 — `addVoiceReminder` keeps the legacy raw-time shape (design §4.1) — accepted.** The elder's one-off voice reminder is not a family editor surface; NFR-MSR-006 requires legacy forms to remain representable, and the ack resolver's rule 3 handles slot-nil candidates without inference. The choice is recorded in the design, not silent.

**n-5 — Evidence capture surfaces should announce which entry they will bind (design §8.1, shared-slot case).** With context-bound capture and no image classification, the only protection against the elder photographing medication B while medication A's dose surface is up is the FR-D01 challenge naming A's medication. design-l2 should require the capture affordance to show/speak the bound medication name (the surfaces already carry it), so the binding is visible at capture time, not only at the challenge.

## Section 23 risk judgments (reviewer assessment)

1. **Calendar-channel content extension (§9.3)** — BLOCKING FOR IMPLEMENT, NOT FOR L1: content class is disclosed (`calendarShare.consent.body` names medicine names explicitly), but the invitee deviation is not; M-1 fixes the treatment.
2. **Simple-ack behaviour change (§7.2)** — accepted; required by FR-MSR-007's failure scenario; single-pending case unchanged (n-1).
3. **Retime refusal for slot entries (§11)** — accepted as direction (deterministic, non-interpretive); the visibility gap is m-5.
4. **Honest-stub change (§9.2)** — accepted and required: FR-MSR-016/018 forbid the silent-success stub; the design plans the explicit `PushOutcome`, the per-contact mapping to `success: false` + errorCode, and the notifier honesty suite. Existing tests that assert stub success must flip with it (covered by the design's suite plan, §17).
5. **Evidence delivery boundary (§8.1, OD-8)** — accepted: FR-MSR-013's delivery discipline is conditional ("where evidence does leave the device"); evidence does not leave the device in this feature, the state is surfaced as "not yet delivered", and the FR-D04b discipline is staked to the transport's landing. This satisfies the requirement as written.
6. **Dose-line composition rule 2 (§4.6)** — accepted: "1 tablet, 500 mg" preserves both the structured quantity and the scanned strength; dropping either loses data the family saw. Separator/copy deferred to implementation is appropriate.
7. **`withScheduleTimes` copy trap (§11)** — verified real (`Models.swift` :144 rebuilds memberwise): the warning and the migration-test obligation must ride into plan-tasks and implementation as a named trap.
8. **Slot words never match legacy (slot-nil) entries (§7.2 rule 3)** — accepted: conservative, deterministic, and consistent with OD-5's no-inference default; the clarification walk is the honest fallback.

## Required resolutions carried out of this review (for design-l2 / implement)

1. **M-1 (major):** resolve the calendar invitee/disclosure mismatch — align significant-event invitees with the shipped suppression rule, or amend the consent copy in both locales to disclose the significant-event audience. Until resolved, no surface may claim the current consent text covers the deviation.
2. **m-1:** give the mapping-change re-arm pass an explicit outcome (or a settings-surface error line) so skipped arming is honestly surfaced, not event-only.
3. **m-2:** state the duplicate-entry rule for slot-model saves in the save plumbing row.
4. **m-3:** pin the hour-to-slot conversion table for scanned schedules at the level the parser emits.
5. **m-4:** pin the "no" behaviour of the multi-candidate clarification walk and test it.
6. **m-5:** decide the retime-refusal's family visibility (surface it or record the quiet revert) and name its observability event in §12's vocabulary.
